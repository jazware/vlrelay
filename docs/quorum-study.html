Quorum replication for vlRelay

Keep the recent log in three replicas, emit an event once two of them hold it, and write the bucket every 30-60 s in big batches with the host cursors in the same commit. The bucket bill drops from ~$2.5k a month to a few dollars, and a 3-node relay lands at ~$15-35 a month on small VPSes.

Every number comes from scripts/cost_model.py --quorum, labeled measured, from code or assumed in docs/quorum.md. Today's load is ~350 events/s. Totals assume 10 full-firehose consumers and R2 after its free tier.

The write path

One stream log with one leader. Each node keeps its share of the PDS websockets and verifies their frames (most of the CPU). Verified events go to the leader, which checks the chain, applies, assigns the next seq and replicates to both followers. The commit point is two of three nodes holding the entry durably in their local commitlog (the recommended shape), and nothing is emitted before it.

With 3 nodes and RF 3 every node is in every replica set anyway, so per-shard leaders would only spread the leader's ~2 cores of extra work at 100x. One log drops the k-way merge, the watermarks and the seqck/ renumbering. Seqs are a plain counter assigned in one place.

Seqs and the emit point

The leader assigns seqs at append. Entries carry (epoch, seq), and a follower only accepts an entry whose predecessor it holds with the same epoch. Nothing reaches any consumer before a quorum holds it.

  • Every flush manifest carries a ceiling R = F + three flush intervals at the sized rate (~86k seqs today at 30 s). The leader never emits above the last committed R.
  • A leader change with a quorum alive continues densely. Uncommitted entries in the adopted tail keep their seqs, since nobody saw them. Entries only the dead leader held were never emitted, and the host owners resend them.
  • After a lost quorum, seqs resume at R + 1. Consumers see a jump forward, never a rewind. Events since the last flush come back from the PDSes under new seqs, so a consumer may see a commit again with the same rev, which sync 1.1 consumers already drop.

The flush and recovery

The manifest is the only object recovery trusts, and it's written last with a CAS. Host cursors reach it through the log itself: host owners send acked cursors to the leader as log entries, and a manifest takes the latest cursor entry per host at or below F. An event counted in a cursor was committed before that cursor entry was appended, so cursors can't get ahead of the flushed log. The DID state is sealed at exactly F for the same reason.

Leadership, epochs and fencing

vlpds's leases fence the bucket. They don't fence memory: a zombie leader whose bucket writes all fail could still get a follower to ack an entry and emit it. So the bucket's CAS picks the leader of each epoch, and each follower's promised epoch fences the replication.

A minority never recovers on its own, since a node that can't reach the others can't tell "they're dead" from "I'm cut off". Recovery is automatic once a quorum is reachable again. Rejoining nodes report their last (epoch, seq), truncate where they differ from the leader and fetch the rest from its local segments or the bucket. Replacing a box goes learner, caught up, flush barrier, CAS of the new member set, with a pause under a second.

A quorum waits only for the nearest follower, so the third replica can sit farther away for free. Hetzner Cloud's spread placement groups guarantee distinct hosts. OVH VPSes have no anti-affinity, so put them in three locations.

Memory only, or a local commitlog

Recommendation: quorum plus a local NVMe commitlog, fsynced with group commit before the ack (Scylla's batch mode). The failures a 3-box cluster really sees are a bad deploy, a shared kernel or OOM bug and a DC power event. Memory alone turns each into a re-ingest and a seq jump. The commitlog turns them into a normal takeover, costs nothing on these hosts, adds a millisecond or two on a VPS, and is the single node's WAL anyway.

Catch-up and backfill

Reads come from the ring in memory (512 MB, ~290 s today), then local disk (every node keeps at least an hour, a VPS-1 ~4 h, an AX42 all 72 h), then the bucket. Segments are 64 MiB now, read with 8 MiB ranged GETs, so a 24 h replay from R2 is ~12k GETs (~$0.004) where the old 4-event segments took ~7.2M ($3). In memory-only the ring must also hold the unflushed tail, up to ~16 GB at 100x with a 60 s flush. With a commitlog the tail is on disk and the ring is just a cache.

Single node

The same flush and re-ingest model with a local NVMe WAL as the durability and emit point: events are emitted once fsynced, with ~2 ms group commit. A process crash or power loss replays the WAL with no re-ingest and no jump. Only losing the disk falls back to the bucket, the re-ingest and the jump to R + 1. The bucket isn't needed to run, but a state-and-cursors bucket turns a lost disk from a gap into a re-ingest, and at a 60 s flush it fits in R2's free tier.

Risks and what to prototype first

Risks

  • Log matching and truncation are new to this codebase and are where correctness bugs would live.
  • Sealing the DID state at exactly F needs SlateDB to never flush a memtable on its own between barriers.
  • VPS fsyncs may not reach power-loss-protected media.
  • Shared vCPUs: a noisy neighbour on the leader is firehose latency.
  • A lost quorum sends repeated commits, and consumers that don't check revs would double-count.
  • The PDS replay window, the RAM baseline and the Hetzner Cloud prices are assumed. Nothing quorum-specific is measured.

Prototype order

  1. Replication with quorum ack and the promise round, with kill -9 and partition chaos and a checker that every emitted seq survives every takeover.
  2. The commitlog with group-commit fsync, and the ack latency on the real hosts (an OVH VPS-2 and an AX42).
  3. The flush: state sealed at F, segments, cursor entries, the manifest CAS, with a crash between every step.
  4. Bucket recovery, the jump to R + 1 and the re-ingest storm at 10x against fakepds.
  5. Membership change at a flush barrier, and a box replacement under load.
  6. Wire Store::counted in and run an hour at today's rate to check the request table.
